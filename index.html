Who needs what in California

March 20–27, San Diego to the Bay Area, with a Ventura pelagic and inland detours. Rare birds first, then the birds you can only get here.

Group passcode

Ask Noah for the passcode. You only need to enter it once on this device.

Target birds

Sorted by tier, then by how many people need each bird. Common, widespread birds are hidden by default — you'll get most of them along the way.

Rare reports

eBird's notable sightings from the last 14 days in every county on the route, plus anything the group adds to the rarity log. Strays often stay only a day or two, so check how many days a bird has been reported and when it was last seen.

Rarity log

Heard about a rare bird on the route? Add it here. From March 10, sightings from the last 7 days go to the top of everyone's target list.

Hotspots

Each hotspot is scored by the needed birds seen there during March 20–27 in past years, weighted by tier and by how many people need them. Suggested stops are picked so each one adds birds the earlier stops don't already cover.

Pelagic day

Your one shot at the seabirds, out of Ventura Harbor into the Santa Barbara Channel. Late-March likelihoods are general guidance — your boat captain's recent trip reports are better.

The group

Add each person's eBird life list. Uploading again under the same name replaces their old list. Only species names are saved — no dates, places or checklist numbers.

Add a life list

In eBird: open My eBird, choose Life List (World or ABA), and use the download link on that page to get a CSV file.

Trip data

Bar chart files from eBird tell the app how often each bird is seen during your trip week. Upload them once and everyone in the group sees the results.

Upload eBird bar charts

  1. Open a county or hotspot bar chart on eBird (links below).
  2. Scroll to the bottom and click Download Histogram Data.
  3. Drop the downloaded .txt files here. You can do several at once.
